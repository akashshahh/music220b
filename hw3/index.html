Music 220b - HW3: Breakcore Drum Machine

Akash Shah

Milestone

An interactive breakcore drum machine built in ChucK with a 16-step sequencer and a breathy pad synth layered underneath.

The sequencer works by looping through 16 steps at sixteenth-note intervals (~170 BPM). Each drum instrument has its own pattern which is an array of 16 probability/velocity values. On each step, if the value is greater than zero, the corresponding sample fires at that velocity. This means patterns aren't just on/off but they have dynamics with ghost notes at lower velocities and accents at higher ones. Each instrument has 4 preset patterns that can be swapped in live, and samples get slight random pitch/velocity variation on each hit to keep things from sounding too mechanical.

The mutation system picks 2–4 random steps in a pattern and flips them. If a step has a hit, it removes it; if it's empty, it adds one at a random velocity. This produces subtle variations on the current pattern instead of generating a completely new pattern, so you can evolve the groove without it being jarring. Full randomize, by contrast, regenerates every pattern from scratch with different density targets (kicks sparse, hats dense, etc.). Didn't rly do this in the recording cause it doesn't sound good every time I've tried it.

The pad synth is 7 voices of detuned saw oscillators mixed with noise through a low-pass filter with a slow LFO sweep, producing big 13th chords that cycle through a shuffleable progression.

Everything is controlled live via keyboard: number keys and letter keys switch between drum pattern presets, z fully randomizes all patterns, x subtly mutates them, c resets to defaults, g toggles the pad on/off, t shuffles the chord progression order, and space triggers a manual crash hit.

In this recording I'm just running the program and messing around with it live, mutating the drum patterns several times, switching between different kick/snare/hat presets, toggling the pad chords on and off, and shuffling the chord progression. You can also hear me hitting the crash on the spacebar at various points; it's pretty hard to land that exactly on beat with a keyboard, which you can hear in a few spots where it's slightly off.

For the final project, I want to explore having the patterns algorithmically mutate at a constant rate on their own, so the beat is always evolving without any input. The keyboard controls would then just be for switching between different patterns that are actively being generated, rather than triggering changes manually.
